# ALFREDO
## Official Rules of the Card Game

### 1. Objective

The objective of Alfredo is to finish with the **lowest total score**.

Players keep their cards face down and try to remember their values. During the game, players can exchange cards, use special abilities, and react quickly when another player discards a card they believe they also have.

When a player believes they have the lowest score, they can call **“Alfredo!”**, triggering the final round.

### 2. Players and Equipment

**Players**
- The standard game is designed for 4 players.
- If playing with more than 4 players, use
$$
\text{Number of decks} = \left\lceil \frac{\text{Number of players}}{4} \right\rceil
$$
 standard decks.

**Cards**
- Use a standard 52-card poker deck.
- Remove all Jokers.
- With more than 1 deck, combine both decks into one draw pile.

**Card values**

| Card | Point value |
|---|---:|
| Ace | 1 |
| 2–10 | Printed value |
| Jack | 11 |
| Queen | 12 |
| Black King (♠, ♣) | 13 |
| Red King (♥, ♦) | 0 |

Card values determine each player's score. Special abilities are separated from point values.

### 3. Setting Up the Game

1. Shuffle the deck.
2. Deal **4 face-down cards to each player**.
3. Each player can look at exactly one of their own cards.
4. Return the card to its original position, keeping it face down.
5. Place the remaining cards face down in the centre to form the draw pile.
6. Establish a playing order, usually clockwise.

Players must not reveal their cards to one another. Remembering card identities and positions is an important part of the game.

The discard pile starts empty and is created as cards are discarded during play.

### 4. A Normal Turn

Players take turns in the established order.

On their turn, a player chooses one of the following sources for a card:

**Option A: Draw from the deck**
- Draw the top card of the draw pile.
- Look at it privately.

**Option B: Take from the discard pile (if cards are present)**
- Take any one card from the discard pile, except an Ace.
- Cards in the discard pile are face up, so their identities are public.

The player then decides what to do with the selected card.

**Keep the card**
1. Choose one of your existing face-down cards.
2. Replace it with the selected card, placing the selected card face down in its place.
3. Put the replaced card face up on the discard pile.
4. Resolve any special ability belonging to the discarded card.

**Discard the drawn card instead**

The player may choose not to keep the drawn card and instead discard it directly. Any special ability belonging to that discarded card is then ignored.

A card taken from the discard pile must be exchanged with one of the player's existing cards; it cannot immediately be returned to the discard pile.

Unless a special ability or penalty changes the number of cards a player holds, a normal exchange does not change the player's card count.

### 5. Special Cards

Special abilities activate when the relevant card is discarded from the player face down cards.

#### Ace — Give a Card

When an Ace is discarded, its player chooses one player, including themselves, to receive an additional card.

- Take the top card from the draw pile.
- Give it to the chosen player face down.
- The recipient adds it to their existing cards without replacing any card.
- The recipient's card count increases by one.

Aces cannot be picked up from the discard pile.

#### Jack — Swap Cards

When a Jack is discarded, its player chooses two different players and swaps one face-down card belonging to each.

- Each selected card stays face down.
- Neither selected card may be looked at during the swap.
- The players involved do not exchange their entire hands; only the two selected cards change ownership.

A player may choose themselves as one of the two players.

#### Queen — Look at a Card

When a Queen is discarded, its player may privately look at one face-down card belonging to any player, including themselves.

- Look at exactly one card.
- Do not reveal its identity to other players.
- Return it face down to the same position.

#### Red King — Zero Points

Both the King of Hearts and the King of Diamonds are worth **0 points**.

They have no special ability.

### 6. Reacting to Another Player's Discard

After a player discards a card, the other players have an opportunity to react before the next player's turn begins.

A player who believes they have a card of the same rank may attempt to discard it immediately.

**A correct reaction**
- The player reveals the card they are attempting to discard so the match can be checked.
- The rank must match the card that prompted the reaction; suits do not matter.
- If correct, the card is placed face up on the discard pile.
- If the discarded card has a special ability, that ability is resolved unless the game is in the final Alfredo round.

**An incorrect reaction**

If the attempted card does not match the required rank:

1. The player must keep the attempted card.
2. The card is returned face down to its place.
3. The player draws one additional card from the draw pile.
4. The player's total number of cards increases by one.

The reaction opportunity ends when the next player's turn begins. Multiple players may react during this opportunity.

### 7. Calling Alfredo

A player who believes they have the lowest score can declare **“Alfredo!”**

The declaration initiates the final round.

Once Alfredo has been called:

- Special card abilities no longer work.
- Players continue taking turns in the usual order.
- Every player other than the caller receives exactly one final turn.
- The caller does not receive another turn.
- The final turn belongs to the player who acted immediately before the caller in the normal playing order.

For example, with four players in the order A → B → C → D, if player B calls Alfredo, players C, D, and A each receive one final turn, in that order. Player A takes the final turn because A acted immediately before B in the normal order.

At the end of the final turn, all players reveal their cards and calculate their totals.

The normal matching-reaction rule remains in effect during the final round, but special abilities are disabled.

### 8. Calculating Scores

At the end of the round, each player adds together the point values of all cards they hold, including any additional cards gained during the game.

Let the caller's card total be the caller's score before the Alfredo adjustment.

There are three possible outcomes.

#### Case A: The caller has the uniquely lowest total

- The caller receives **−10 points**.
- Every other player receives their own card total.

The caller's score is −10, not −10 plus their card total.

#### Case B: The caller does not have the lowest total

- The caller receives their card total **plus 10 points**.
- The player or players with the lowest card total receive **0 points**.
- Every other player receives their own card total.

#### Case C: The caller ties for the lowest total

If the caller has the same lowest card total as another player, the Alfredo adjustment does not apply.

- Every player receives their own card total.
- No player receives the −10-point reward or the +10-point penalty.

### 9. Example Scoring

Suppose there are four players:

| Player | Card total |
|---|---:|
| Alice (caller) | 8 |
| Bob | 15 |
| Charlie | 21 |
| Diana | 12 |

Alice has the uniquely lowest total, so she receives −10 points. Bob receives 15, Charlie receives 21, and Diana receives 12.

Now suppose Alice has 14 points, Bob has 9, Charlie has 21, and Diana has 12. Alice did not have the lowest total, so she receives 14 + 10 = 24 points. Bob receives 0, Charlie receives 21, and Diana receives 12.

Finally, suppose Alice and Bob both have 9 points, the lowest total. They are tied for the lowest score, so everyone receives their own card total without any Alfredo adjustment.

### 10. Winning the Game

Players may play a single round or agree on a multi-round match before starting.

In a multi-round match:
- Record every player's score after each round.
- Add each new round's score to that player's cumulative score.
- Negative scores are allowed.
- At the end of the agreed match, the player with the **lowest cumulative score wins**.

### 11. Important Rules to Remember

- Keep your cards face down unless an ability or rule allows them to be viewed.
- Only look at cards when permitted.
- Special abilities activate when the relevant card is discarded, not when it is drawn or held.
- Aces may not be taken from the discard pile.
- An incorrect reaction increases the player's card count by one.
- An Ace's ability also increases the target's card count by one.
- Both red Kings are worth zero points.
- Once Alfredo is called, special abilities are disabled for the rest of the round.
- The player immediately before the caller in the normal playing order takes the final turn.
- The caller only receives the −10-point reward if they have the uniquely lowest total.
- If the caller ties for the lowest total, everyone scores their own card total.
